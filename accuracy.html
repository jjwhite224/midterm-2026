03 / WHY THESE NUMBERS MATTER

A warning about trust. A test of accuracy.

The 2026 polling story begins with two different questions: Were public surveys genuine, and did genuine surveys accurately measure the electorate? Reporting about fabricated polls raised concerns about the first. Pre-election discussion of weighting adjustments raised questions about the second.

This interactive audit addresses a narrower, measurable question: among contests with comparable public surveys, how far did the polling margin differ from reported election results? The data alone cannot show why an error happened or establish that a particular weighting change caused it.

Selection

Baseline uses questions ending October 4–November 2, 2026, excludes flagged partisan/internal surveys and known fabricated pollsters, and prefers likely-voter samples. Original survey questions are deduplicated.

Calculation

Both polling and results margins are normalized to the major-party vote: 100 × (D−R)/(D+R). Signed error = election margin − polling margin; absolute error is the magnitude.

Two evidence tiers

Headline summaries require two distinct pollsters in a race. Exploratory figures include single-pollster contests and are not equivalent evidence.

Limitations

Most districts lack qualifying polls, the dataset is not a random sample, candidate lineups can be unusual, published questions may be correlated, and the election returns are not certified.
