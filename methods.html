01 / DEFINITIONS

What is a polling error?

A poll measures respondents' preferences before the election; an election result counts reported votes afterward. We compare their margins using a consistent Democratic-minus-Republican, two-party denominator for eligible races:

Survey margin = 100 × (Democratic survey share − Republican survey share) ÷ (Democratic survey share + Republican survey share)

Election margin = 100 × (Democratic votes − Republican votes) ÷ (Democratic votes + Republican votes)

Signed error = election margin − survey polling average. A positive result means Democratic performance was understated; a negative result means Republican performance was understated.

Absolute error = |signed error|, the size of the miss regardless of party.

These values are percentage-point differences in a margin, not the percent of respondents who were wrong, not forecast probabilities, and not an estimate of voter turnout.

02 / RECORDS & ELIGIBILITY

How we choose comparable surveys.

Election and dates

The default window includes 2026 general-election surveys with fieldwork ending October 4 through November 2. Alternative windows of 21 and 45 days are calculated separately.

Matchup and population

Match the reported Democratic and Republican general-election candidates conservatively. Prefer likely-voter questions; use registered voters or adults only if no eligible likely-voter questions exist for the race.

Deduplication and provenance

Retain pollster, source URL, fieldwork end date, original question ID and survey ID. Multiple questions from one survey must not be counted as independent surveys. Exclude altered ranked-choice variants where comparisons are not straightforward.

Exclusion criteria

The default analysis excludes known fabricated polls, flagged partisan/internal polls, and races with more than 5% of votes outside the two major parties or without unique comparable D/R candidates.

Two levels of evidence

Headline comparisons require at least two different polling organizations; a separate exploratory tier permits one. Neither is a probability statement or representative national sample.

What remains missing

Five final University of New Hampshire House polls have been added after comparing full university releases against original candidate figures and dates. These cover NH-01, NH-02, ME-01, ME-02, and VT-00, with original percentages preserved in a separate attributed file. Other unverified late polls remain excluded. The public race catalog contains all 470 contests, but most lack qualifying polling. Missing evidence stays missing—it is not recorded as zero error.

04 / THE BIG LIMITATION

These are not all 470 races.

Campaigns and publishers release district polling unevenly, often focusing on competitive contests. A race with several published surveys is not a randomly selected member of the House. If a summary gives each qualifying race equal weight, it describes the races in that summary—not all voters, all districts, or the entire industry's error.

Even comparisons across different selection rules change which races are included. A smaller mean signed error after adding partisan polls does not, by itself, show that partisan polls are better or worse. A negative or positive error does not establish its cause. Weighting, nonresponse, turnout, survey timing, and late decisions are possible explanations to evaluate with additional evidence.

The November 19 results file is still unofficial. A full publication-grade audit needs state-certified returns, verification of original releases for a sample of races, a completed November 1–2 House poll review, and harmonized candidate definitions for unusual contests.

05 / REPRODUCIBILITY AND AI USE

Code, calculations, and disclosure.

The source code, data snapshots, and GitHub Actions workflows are publicly available. Python assembles survey comparisons and error calculations; JavaScript and SVG render charts and filtering entirely in the browser. The site does not generate results from live browser API calls or substitute model forecasts for missing polls.

ChatGPT and Codex assisted with dataset collection scripts, transformation logic, frontend implementation, accessibility, testing, and editing. Automated tests check file structure and scenario invariants. AI-generated code and analysis require independent review; AI assistance is not a source of polling data, an election result, or certification.
